Set enclosures

Sets take no space in the arrange trees: place the members with place and leave room for the outline and label. Palettes

figure.themeOverrides.palette: <name> recolors a theme with four colors from a popular Color Hunt palette: panels (group fill) take the palest, filled and tinted nodes and the gray tone the tint, solid fills and emphasis the accent (deepened for white text), and text, frames and edges the ink (the darkest color, darkened to 9:1 on white when it is paler). Every text keeps at least 4.5:1 on the fill it sits on, so the same figure reads in each palette. The tones are derived from the palette too (#423): gray is its neutral, and red, green, blue and the rest keep their hue but take the palette's lightness and chroma (the row of tinted tone boxes), so a figure in a palette stays in one family of colors; an explicit themeOverrides.tones color wins. Sizes and fonts stay the theme's. The figure is the same in every palette: only palette changes in the source. One figure, every theme × profile

The same source rendered with --theme and --profile overrides: nothing is duplicated. Profiles do not scale a figure unless its source opts into fit: scale. The paper profiles share their spacing and differ only in the width limit that the geometry lint checks, so their drawings match. A profile whose minimum font size is larger than a theme's font sizes rejects the figure before rendering (profile/font-too-small): presentation-16x9 requires 14pt text, so it accepts only the slides theme, and the other themes are not rendered there by design. Theme and profile stay independent; for slides use theme: slides with profile: presentation-16x9. A figure that exceeds a page limit is still drawn, with the geometry error below it: slides doubles the text and the presentation profile doubles the spacing, so this tall top-to-bottom figure is taller than a slide. On paper-single, the pipeline with slides fits the page but reports two collision warnings (geometry/label-collision and geometry/edge-crosses-label), distinct from the presentation profile's height error. A slide needs a shallow figure: fewer layers, or several figures.
