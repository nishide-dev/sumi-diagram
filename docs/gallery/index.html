Tones

A node or group picks a color category with tone (gray, red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, pink); the theme decides the color. color fills each tone with its own pastel hue. The monochrome themes map the tones to three gray steps so a figure still prints in grayscale; tones in the same step look alike there. A tone changes only colors, over the variant.

Dimmed groups

A group with dim: true is drawn pale with everything inside it: its nested groups, nodes, caption and the edges whose two ends are inside. Text and lines take the theme's dim gray and fills move toward white, so tones stay recognizable; sizes, dashes and the layout do not change. The group keeps its own variant and tone. The dashed line from the left panel ends inside the dimmed one and is drawn as written.

Source: dim.sumi.yaml

Outlined tones and edge tones

With variant: outlined a tone colors the outline and the text of a node instead of its fill (left column; without a tone, tone gray's). An edge's tone colors its line, arrowheads and label. Both use the tone's line color: a dark shade of the hue in color and paper, readable as text (at least 4.5:1 on white), and one of three gray steps in the monochrome themes. The right column shows the same tones as fills (default variant) for comparison.

One figure, every theme × profile

The same source rendered with --theme and --profile overrides: nothing is duplicated. Profiles never scale a figure. The paper profiles share their spacing and differ only in the width limit that the geometry lint checks, so their drawings match. A profile whose minimum font size is larger than a theme's font sizes rejects the figure before rendering (profile/font-too-small): presentation-16x9 requires 14pt text, so it accepts only the slides theme, and the other themes are not rendered there by design. Theme and profile stay independent; for slides use theme: slides with profile: presentation-16x9. A figure that exceeds a page limit is still drawn, with the geometry error below it: slides doubles the text and the presentation profile doubles the spacing, so this tall top-to-bottom figure is taller than a slide. A slide needs a shallow figure: fewer layers, or several figures.
